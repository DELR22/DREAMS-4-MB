Importance Measurement

How Importance Is Measured

The canvas is a four-quadrant plane. Its center, where the X and Y axes meet, is the heart of the mood, and every bubble is placed somewhere around it.

Distance from the center. A bubble placed exactly on the center scores 100. The score falls the farther the bubble moves away, reaching 0 at any of the four corners. Direction does not matter — left, right, up or down all count the same, and there are no negative values. Only how far the bubble sits from the center changes its score. The faint rings on the canvas mark where the score drops to 75, 50 and 25.

Size of the bubble. The bigger a bubble is drawn, the heavier or more dominant that element is meant to feel in the mood board.

Importance Measurement (IM) multiplies the two: the bubble's score × its radius. Bubbles of the same type are added together, and the percentage shown is that type's share of everything mapped on the canvas — the higher the share, the more that element should define the mood board.

The Space typed in Session Setup names what the mood board represents. It is context, not a bubble, so it earns no IM.
